# Data sources and collection checklist

Contents: what to collect · where to look · handling gaps

## What to collect

For the company (and each peer), with the date and currency of each figure:

- Share price, number of shares outstanding, market capitalisation
- Financial debt (and lease liabilities), cash and equivalents, minority interests, preferred equity, to compute enterprise value
- Revenue, EBITDA (state whether before or after lease costs), net income, earnings per share, free cash flow, all trailing twelve months or last fiscal year
- Book value of equity (for P/B and ROE)
- Dividend per share
- Revenue growth and EBITDA or operating margin (to explain peer differences)
- Historical multiples: EV/EBITDA and P/E for the last 5 to 10 years, either from a data provider or estimated from year-end prices and reported figures

## Where to look

Search for the figures with the company name or ISIN plus the metric, then open the best result and read the numbers from the page rather than from the search snippet.

1. A connected financial-data tool, if the user has one. Use it first.
2. The company's investor-relations page: annual and half-year reports, which give debt, cash, EBITDA, net income and leases.
3. Exchange and regulator filings (e.g. SEC filings for US companies, the company register or exchange announcements for European ones).
4. Reputable financial-data and market sites for prices, market cap and historical multiples. Check at least two sources for any figure that drives the conclusion.

Resolving an ISIN: search for the ISIN string by itself and confirm company name, country and exchange from the result before using any other data.

## Handling gaps

- Missing figure: write "not found" and say which ratio this blocks.
- Conflicting sources: show both values and the reason if known (different definitions, dates, currencies).
- No historical series: estimate from annual data and label it "approximate", or state that history was unavailable.
- Peers with thin data: drop them and pick another, or say the peer set is smaller than planned.
- Never fill gaps from memory or by estimating peer numbers. An honest gap is better than a plausible-looking guess.
